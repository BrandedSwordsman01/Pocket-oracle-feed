# AEGIS 2.0: research findings

Full numbers: [aegis2_backtest_results.md](aegis2_backtest_results.md) (generated by `research/run_aegis.py`, ~2 minutes).
Mathematics and corrections: [aegis2_mathematics.md](aegis2_mathematics.md). Risks: [aegis2_execution_risks.md](aegis2_execution_risks.md).

## Verdict

**Profitability is not demonstrated.** AEGIS is a correct, well-tested settlement pricer, but it contains **no statistically significant
information beyond Kalshi's own prices**. The only positive trading result (strategy G) has a bootstrap confidence interval that includes zero,
and it nearly disappears with a one-minute execution delay.

## Answers to the 11 questions

**1. Is AEGIS better calibrated than Kalshi market probabilities?**
No. On the untouched test period (Nov 7–22, 17,033 tradeable rows): market Brier 0.1780 / log loss 0.5294. AEGIS alone (E) scores 0.1841 / 0.5466,
**significantly worse** (log-loss gap +0.017, day-block 95% CI +0.010 … +0.025). The best density model is D (GARCH-t × diurnal):
PIT tail mass 0.102 vs the ideal 0.10, KS 0.027. It is still miscalibrated (critical value 0.021), and it is still worse than the market.

**2. Does the model contain genuinely incremental information?**
Not detectably. Pooled with the market (F, ω = 0.23 chosen on validation) it scores log loss 0.5292 vs 0.5294: a difference of −0.0002 with
95% CI −0.0018 … +0.0015. Re-calibrating the market itself (M) gives −0.0005, CI −0.0014 … +0.0004. Any incremental information is below what 16 test days can resolve.

**3. Are any differences large enough to overcome transaction costs?**
Not reliably. G (pooled, conservative bound, θ = 1¢ chosen on validation) made **+1.58¢/contract over 469 test trades (+$74 on 10-lots)**,
but the day-block 95% CI for net P&L is **−$65 … +$243**. Validation was similar (+2.12¢). With a one-minute-delayed fill it drops to +0.28¢.
That pattern suggests part of the apparent edge comes from minute-close quotes that would already have moved.

**4. How frequently do real cross-market dominance violations occur?**
Almost never. Of **1,623** minutes in which a 15-minute window and the hourly ladder shared a settlement, **1** had an after-fee dominance pair.
Within the hourly ladder alone: **0 of 4,467** minutes.

**5. Are those opportunities actually executable?**
Unproven, and probably not. The single signal came from minute-close snapshots of two separate books (not synchronous). There's no historical
depth, the legs would be filled sequentially, and the 15M + hourly pair carries the official data-outage risk (15M resolves NO while the hourly settles later).
The paper collector now journals synchronous live books every minute to measure this properly.

**6. Is the 15-minute or hourly market more promising?**
15-minute, marginally. On hourly KXBTCD, AEGIS is worse than the market in all three splits (test Brier 0.1352 vs 0.1311). The cross-market consensus
signal (X, the hourly-implied fair value for the 15M contract) was *less* accurate than the 15M quote itself (test Brier 0.1722 vs 0.1617).
So the 15-minute book is the better-informed one, and neither offers a demonstrated edge.

**7. Which time-to-expiration periods perform best?**
5–9 minutes before close: +0.75¢/contract (F at θ = 0, 444 test trades). 10–14 minutes: −1.39¢. 2–4 minutes: −0.15¢. None is significant.
Calibration differences by horizon are below 0.0003 Brier.

**8. Are maker or taker orders more promising after costs?**
Taker strategies lose to fees and spread (fees were $56–166 per strategy on test). Maker economics would avoid most of the fee (0.0175 vs 0.07) and earn
the spread, which is why the roadmap ranks makers first. But **maker fills cannot be backtested** without queue and depth history. Following the brief,
no maker result is claimed.

**9. What is the realistic paper-trading return and drawdown?**
Historical simulation for G on test: +$74.19 on ~$2,300 of cumulative contract cost (16 days, 469 trades, 10-lots), max drawdown $83.
That is statistically indistinguishable from zero. With conservative Kelly sizing and a $25 daily stop: −$66.72. Live paper trading
(`research/paper_trader.py`) was verified end to end but has run only minutes, so there is no forward result yet.

**10. Which proposed improvements failed?**
- **Jumps (model E):** worse PIT than GARCH-t (KS 0.054 vs 0.027). Empirical jumps on Gaussian diffusion under-fill the centre and over-fill the shoulders.
- **GARCH/diurnal vs constant vol for trading:** no help. Calibrated D and E lost (−1.0 and −1.2¢ test), while B came out +0.1¢.
- **Conservative bootstrap bounds:** **failed coverage in 9 of 20 bins**, mostly the YES side. The bounds capture calibration-map noise
  but not the regime shift between train and test. That is a key reason no strategy should be trusted live.
- **Cross-market consensus (X):** the hourly-implied fair value was a worse predictor than the 15M price.
- **Kelly sizing:** turned a small positive into a loss.
- **Not attempted:** a latent 3-state regime model (no out-of-sample case for it; GARCH persistence 0.999 already models clustering) and
  Deribit option-surface priors (only the 30-day DVOL index is free, and it hurt in the earlier study).

**11. What evidence would justify eventually considering live trading?**
All of the following:
- (a) **Forward** paper trading with live depth, ≥ 1,000 trades over ≥ 6 weeks, with positive net expectancy after fees,
  realistic latency (signal → order ≥ 1 s) and a 95% CI excluding zero.
- (b) Bound coverage ≥ 90% of bins on the forward data.
- (c) The edge persisting under +1¢ slippage and one-minute-delayed fills.
- (d) For any pair strategy, synchronous two-leg fills observed in the live book, with outage risk sized as a loss.
- (e) Live order-routing tested on Kalshi's demo exchange.

None of these is met today.

## What beat what (identical data and splits)

| Rank (test log loss) | Model |
|---|---|

| 2 | F AEGIS ⊕ market 0.5292 (≈ market) |
| 3 | A market mid 0.5294 |
| 4 | C EWMA Student-t 0.5450 |

| 6 | D GARCH-t × diurnal 0.5460 (best *distribution* by PIT) |
| 7 | E AEGIS (GARCH + jumps) 0.5466 |

**Replacement recommended by the evidence:** for pricing, keep the **simpler GARCH-t × diurnal density (D)** rather than the jump model, and always
**pool with the market** (ω ≈ 0.2). For trading, there is no model to deploy. The app shows AEGIS as a decision aid with PASS as the default outcome.
