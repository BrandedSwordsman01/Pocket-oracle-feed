How this works

    

      
The bet
Kalshi asks a yes/no question about Bitcoin's price at a set time, e.g. "Will Bitcoin be at or above $60,679 at 9:00?"

      
YES / NO
Buy YES if you think the answer is yes, NO if you think it's no. A correct contract pays $1; a wrong one pays $0.

      
Price = chance
A YES at 57¢ means the market thinks there's about a 57% chance. You pay 57¢ to win $1 (43¢ profit) or lose your 57¢.

      
The line
The price Bitcoin has to beat. For 15-minute bets it's where Bitcoin was when that 15-minute window opened.

      
Our model
Our own estimate of the chance, based on how much Bitcoin usually moves at this time of day. It is mixed with Kalshi's price because the market is usually right.

      
Good bet / No good bet
"Good bet" only when even a cautious version of our estimate beats the price after Kalshi's fee. Most of the time the price is fair and the answer is "no good bet" — that's normal.

      
Fees
Kalshi charges up to about 2¢ per contract. Costs shown already include it.

      
Likely range (dotted lines)
Where Bitcoin will probably be at the next close: 9 times out of 10 inside the outer lines.

      
Auto-bot
Places small bets for you only when the model says "good bet", with daily loss limits. Paper = pretend money, Live = real money.

      
Honest note
No strategy here is proven to make money. Bet only what you can afford to lose.
